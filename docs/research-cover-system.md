# Alesvia — Research Cover System

## Purpose

This document defines the first visual system for Alesvia research covers.

The goal is to make research outputs feel consistent, serious, and recognizable without requiring a custom illustration for every article.

---

## Core structure

Use the base research cover template for:

- research posts
- briefings
- reports
- essays
- social preview images for publication drops

Template:

- [research-cover.svg](/C:/axiom/public/brand/templates/research-cover.svg)

---

## Cover hierarchy

Every cover should include:

- Alesvia Research lockup
- title
- short descriptor or subtitle
- output type: `Briefing`, `Report`, or `Essay`
- optional date or initiative label

The title is always the dominant element.

---

## Format distinction

### Briefing

Use for fast, policy-relevant analysis.

Visual emphasis:

- more direct title
- visible output type
- civic teal accent

### Report

Use for structured, longer synthesis.

Visual emphasis:

- more formal spacing
- darker primary ink
- optional secondary line for report scope

### Essay

Use for founder voice and thesis-building.

Visual emphasis:

- more editorial composition
- warmer paper background
- restrained gold accent

---

## Color use

Use the primary identity colors first:

- primary ink: `#16324F`
- accent gold: `#C79B3B`
- paper: `#F4F1EA`

Use secondary accents to avoid repetitive covers:

- civic teal: `#2A7F78`
- pale civic mist: `#DCE7E2`
- policy clay: `#A74E3A`

Do not use all accents on one cover.

---

## Title rules

- keep the title to one or two lines when possible
- avoid tiny subtitles
- do not turn the cover into an abstract poster
- preserve enough whitespace for the output to feel institutional
